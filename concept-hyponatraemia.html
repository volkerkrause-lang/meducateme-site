HYPONATRAEMIA
IS A WATER PROBLEM

Start with one useful idea: serum sodium is mainly a marker of water balance relative to body solute. Then follow the consequences into the brain and back to treatment.

Na⁺
↔ WATER
01 · REFRAME IT

Do not start with sodium loss

Ask first: is there too much water relative to sodium and potassium in the body?

Body soluteMainly sodium and potassium salts
↔
Body waterControlled largely through thirst and ADH
→
[Na⁺]A concentration, not a direct inventory
Commit before reveal

If total body sodium stays similar but water increases, serum sodium will…

02 · TONICITY

Water follows effective osmolality

In hypotonic hyponatraemia, extracellular fluid becomes less concentrated than intracellular fluid.

Serum Na⁺ ↓Relative water excess
→
ECF tonicity ↓Outside becomes more dilute
→
Water enters cellsOsmosis
→
Brain swellingSymptoms depend on speed + severity
Why speed matters: over time, brain cells shed osmolytes and adapt. That reduces swelling—but it also makes overly rapid correction of chronic hyponatraemia dangerous.
03 · CONSEQUENCES

Derive the symptoms

Instead of memorising a list, derive it from cerebral oedema and the rate of change.

Clinical interruption

A child with sodium 121 is awake and talking. Another falls from 138 to 121 in several hours and develops a seizure. Why?

The rate of fall changes how much cerebral adaptation is possible.

Which feature most strongly raises concern for symptomatic cerebral oedema?

04 · FIND THE DRIVER

Ask what ADH is doing

Urine osmolality tells you whether the kidney is appropriately getting rid of free water.

Confirm hypotonicityNot every low sodium is hypotonic
→
Urine osmolalityDilute = ADH off; concentrated = ADH effect
→
Volume + urine NaRefine the mechanism
→
Treat the causeAnd symptoms safely
Flashcard · tap

What should ADH do when plasma becomes hypotonic?

Switch off, allowing excretion of dilute urine—unless a non-osmotic stimulus overrides the system.
Flashcard · tap

Why is a concentrated urine important?

It suggests the kidney is retaining water under ADH influence rather than maximally excreting free water.
