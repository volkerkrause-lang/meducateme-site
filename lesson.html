04 · Return to every finding

MAKE THE CASE
COHERE

A good teaching session should finish by explaining every important feature of the original presentation.

Why the thirst and polyuria?

Hyperglycaemia exceeds renal glucose reabsorptive capacity → glycosuria → osmotic diuresis → water loss → thirst and dehydration.

Why the vomiting and abdominal pain?

Ketosis and acidaemia commonly produce gastrointestinal symptoms; dehydration further worsens the child’s condition.

Why the deep breathing?

The respiratory system compensates for metabolic acidosis by increasing ventilation, lowering pCO₂. The characteristic deep breathing is Kussmaul respiration.

Why can potassium mislead?

Total-body potassium is depleted through urinary losses, but serum potassium may initially be normal or high because insulin deficiency and acidaemia shift potassium extracellularly.

05 · Management follows physiology

TREAT THE
PROBLEMS YOU JUST BUILT

This section teaches the rationale, not a substitute protocol. Exact fluid, insulin, potassium and monitoring decisions must follow current paediatric DKA guidance and local pathways.

Fluids

Restore circulation and replace water deficit carefully while accounting for the risks specific to paediatric DKA.

Insulin

Stops ketogenesis, suppresses lipolysis and reverses the metabolic process causing the acidosis.

Potassium

Anticipate falling serum potassium once insulin is started and acidosis resolves; replace according to measured values and protocol.

Neurological monitoring

Repeated neurological assessment matters because cerebral injury is a feared complication and deterioration requires urgent action.
