Why does the body need cortisol?

SURVIVE
STRESS

Cortisol is not simply a “stress hormone”. It coordinates fuel availability, vascular responsiveness and inflammatory restraint so the organism can function during fasting, infection, trauma and other stress.

Liver↑ gluconeogenesis → preserve circulating glucose
Muscle↑ protein catabolism → amino-acid substrate
Adipose↑ lipolysis → mobilise fuel
VasculaturePermissive support of catecholamine-mediated vascular tone
Immune systemRestrains inflammatory gene expression and cytokine signalling
Bone / growthChronic excess suppresses growth and bone formation

Now break the assembly line

CONGENITAL ADRENAL
HYPERPLASIA

CAH becomes logical once the normal pathway is visible: block an enzyme → product falls → precursor accumulates → cortisol feedback falls → ACTH rises → adrenal stimulation and hyperplasia → substrate is diverted into pathways that remain open.

Precursor ↑ → shunting into available pathways

One final distinction

PRIMARY OR
CENTRAL?

Ask where the axis failed. Primary adrenal failure means the gland cannot answer ACTH. Secondary/tertiary deficiency means ACTH/CRH drive is inadequate; aldosterone is usually relatively preserved because RAAS, not ACTH, is its main regulator.

Primary

Cortisol ↓ · ACTH ↑

± aldosterone ↓ → salt loss / hyperkalaemia

ACTH excess can cause hyperpigmentation.

Central

Cortisol ↓ · ACTH low/inappropriately normal

Aldosterone generally preserved.

No ACTH-driven hyperpigmentation.

What you should now be able to explain

Why the adrenal cortex is zoned; why all steroid hormones start with cholesterol; how ACTH rapidly increases cortisol synthesis; why cortisol deficiency can cause hypoglycaemia and shock; why primary adrenal failure can disturb sodium and potassium; and why 21-hydroxylase deficiency produces androgen excess and sometimes salt wasting.
