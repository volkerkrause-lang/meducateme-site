03 · Fundamental knowledge

What does insulin
actually do?

Before DKA makes sense, insulin has to mean more than “a hormone that lowers glucose”. It is the body's signal that fuel is available.

PancreasGlucose rises → β cells release insulin

Insulin signals the fed state.

→
Whole bodyUse and store fuel

Muscle takes up glucose; liver stores rather than produces it.

→
AdiposeStop mobilising fat

Insulin restrains lipolysis and therefore ketone production.

01

Muscle

Insulin promotes GLUT4-mediated glucose uptake and glycogen synthesis.

02

Liver

Insulin suppresses glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis and favours glycogen formation.

03

Adipose tissue

Insulin suppresses hormone-sensitive lipase, limiting release of free fatty acids.

Take home: insulin is a signal of energy abundance. Its absence makes the body behave metabolically as though it is starving, even while glucose is accumulating in the blood.

05 · Derive the symptoms

Why does Maya
feel like this?

Do not memorise the symptom list. Predict each symptom from the mechanism.

Mechanism question

Why does hyperglycaemia produce both polyuria and intense thirst?

WHY?

Weight loss

Water and urinary calorie loss combine with a catabolic state: lipolysis and proteolysis continue despite extracellular glucose abundance.

WHY?

Vomiting

Ketosis, acidaemia, dehydration and disturbed gastrointestinal function contribute. DKA can mimic an acute abdomen.

WHY?

Abdominal pain

Often accompanies significant ketoacidosis; persistence after metabolic improvement should make you reconsider another abdominal diagnosis.

Take home: the symptoms are visible outputs of the metabolic pathways, not unrelated diagnostic clues.
06 · Derive the signs

The lungs are
trying to help

Deep breathing is not primarily a respiratory disease. It is a compensatory response to metabolic acidosis.

Mechanism question

Why does metabolic acidosis produce Kussmaul breathing?

Acid–base link: ketoacids consume bicarbonate → pH falls → chemoreceptor-driven ventilation rises → PaCO₂ falls → pH is partially defended. Tachycardia, meanwhile, is an expected compensatory response to reduced effective circulating volume.

The potassium trap

Serum potassium is 4.7 mmol/L. What does that tell you about total-body potassium?

Take home: glucose measures one branch, ketones another, and pH/bicarbonate their acid-base consequence. Electrolytes and renal indices tell you what osmotic diuresis and treatment may do next. Imaging is not routine in uncomplicated DKA.
08 · Repair the physiology

Treatment should
feel inevitable

Once you understand what is broken, each intervention has a precise physiological job.

Key concept

Glucose has fallen substantially but ketones and acidosis persist. Why might dextrose be added while insulin continues?

01 · FLUID

Restore effective circulation

Replace intravascular/extracellular losses, support perfusion and renal function, and calculate ongoing replacement using the current paediatric pathway.

02 · INSULIN

Stop ketogenesis

Insulin suppresses lipolysis and hepatic ketone production. Current UK paediatric guidance uses continuous IV insulin after initial fluid therapy and no IV insulin bolus.

03 · POTASSIUM

Expect the serum level to fall

Total-body stores are depleted. Insulin and metabolic recovery move potassium intracellularly, so replacement and serial monitoring are integral to treatment.

04 · DEXTROSE

Keep insulin going safely

When glucose falls before ketoacidosis has resolved, dextrose allows continued insulin without causing hypoglycaemia.

SAFETY

Use the current BSPED/local paediatric DKA pathway for exact fluids, insulin, potassium and monitoring. Neurological deterioration requires urgent senior assessment and treatment; cerebral injury in DKA is multifactorial and should not be reduced to the simplistic idea that “fluids cause cerebral oedema”.

09 · Prove you understand it

Don't chase
the glucose

A seven-year-old with type 1 diabetes has a pump interruption. She is vomiting and breathing deeply. Glucose 14.2 mmol/L, blood ketones 4.8 mmol/L, venous pH 7.19.

Transfer question

Why is the relatively modest glucose falsely reassuring?

Transfer principle: ask whether insulin deficiency is driving ketogenesis and whether that ketogenesis has produced acidosis. This protects you from equating “DKA severity” with the glucose number.
10 · Retrieval and synthesis

Explain DKA
without a list

Explain Maya in a causal chain: normal insulin action → what failed → why each symptom/sign appeared → why each test was chosen → why each treatment works.

Your 60-second explanation

One possible explanation:
Absolute insulin deficiency removes the fed-state signal. Peripheral glucose utilisation falls while hepatic glucose production continues, producing hyperglycaemia. Glycosuria then causes osmotic diuresis, water/electrolyte loss, polyuria, thirst, dehydration and compensatory tachycardia. Simultaneously, loss of insulin restraint on lipolysis delivers free fatty acids to the liver, generating ketones and metabolic acidosis; deep Kussmaul ventilation lowers CO₂ to partially defend pH. We therefore measure glucose, ketones and venous acid-base status to identify the two metabolic branches and their consequence, and electrolytes/renal indices to understand losses and treatment risk. Fluids restore circulation, insulin stops lipolysis and ketogenesis, potassium is anticipated because whole-body stores are depleted, and dextrose may be added so insulin can continue until ketoacidosis resolves.
Five take-homes: DKA is insulin deficiency, not simply high glucose. Hyperglycaemia explains osmotic diuresis; lipolysis explains ketones and acidosis. Serum potassium does not equal body potassium stores. Insulin treats ketogenesis as well as glucose. Treatment is the physiology run in reverse.
Clinical grounding: Exact management must follow the current BSPED paediatric DKA guideline, NICE NG18 and the local paediatric pathway. Educational content only.
