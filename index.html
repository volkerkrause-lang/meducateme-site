03 · How should the system work?

Insulin signals
fuel is available

After a meal, rising blood glucose stimulates pancreatic beta cells. Insulin then coordinates how the body uses and stores that fuel.

SignalBlood glucose rises

Absorbed carbohydrate enters the circulation.

→
SensorBeta cells release insulin

The pancreas detects increased glucose availability.

→
MessageUse glucose; stop making fuel

Insulin creates the coordinated fed state.

01

Muscle and fat

Insulin promotes glucose uptake and storage.

02

Liver

Insulin suppresses glucose production and promotes glycogen formation.

03

Adipose tissue

Insulin suppresses lipolysis, preventing unnecessary ketone production.

The crucial idea: insulin does more than lower glucose. It tells the whole body that it is safe to use carbohydrate and stop producing emergency fuel.
04 · What is causing the problem?

Why is she
breathing deeply?

The lungs are not the source of the problem. They are responding to it.

Mechanism question

What is the best explanation for Maya’s deep, sighing respirations?

Ketone acids lower bicarbonate and pH → chemoreceptors increase ventilation → more CO₂ is exhaled → the fall in CO₂ partially compensates for the metabolic acidosis.

Interpretation question

What is the safest interpretation of the potassium result?

Need a clue?

Separate the potassium concentration in the sampled blood from potassium stores across the whole body.

What about imaging? Imaging does not diagnose uncomplicated DKA and is not routine. Use it only when the presentation suggests an alternative diagnosis or a complication requiring imaging.
08 · Apply your knowledge: treatment

Treat each branch
of the mechanism

Management is safer and easier to remember when each action has a physiological purpose.

First-principles question

Which approach best reflects initial paediatric DKA management?

01 · FLUID

Restore circulation and replace losses carefully

Fluid supports perfusion and renal clearance. Calculate it using the current paediatric pathway rather than an adult protocol.

02 · INSULIN

Switch off ketogenesis

Continuous low-dose insulin suppresses lipolysis, stops new ketone production and enables glucose use. No IV insulin bolus.

03 · POTASSIUM

Anticipate the intracellular shift

Body stores are depleted, while insulin and improving acidosis move potassium into cells. Replace and monitor according to the guideline and renal function.

04 · DEXTROSE

Keep clearing ketones safely

As glucose falls, dextrose may be added so insulin can continue until ketoacidosis resolves.

SAFETY

Repeated neurological observations matter. Headache, unexpected slowing of heart rate, altered behaviour or falling consciousness require urgent senior assessment and treatment for suspected cerebral oedema according to the emergency pathway.

10 · Retrieval and explanation

Can you explain
the whole chain?

Without looking back, explain Maya’s DKA in five linked “because” statements. Then compare your reasoning with the model.

Your explanation

One possible explanation:
Beta-cell destruction caused absolute insulin deficiency. Because insulin’s signal was absent, glucose use fell while hepatic glucose output rose, causing hyperglycaemia. Because filtered glucose remained in the renal tubule, osmotic diuresis caused polyuria, dehydration and thirst. Because insulin no longer restrained lipolysis, the liver produced ketones, causing metabolic acidosis. Because of the acidosis, Maya developed deep Kussmaul breathing to lower carbon dioxide. Fluids restore perfusion, insulin stops ketogenesis, and potassium must be anticipated because total-body stores are depleted even when the initial serum level appears normal.
Clinical grounding: Treatment principles are aligned with the BSPED paediatric DKA guideline and NICE NG18. Educational content only; always use the current local paediatric DKA pathway, senior clinical support and specialist diabetes advice.
