MEDUCATEME · MINI LECTURE
PHYSIOLOGY · 8–10 MINUTES

Insulin is a
fed-state signal

Move through this like a conversation rather than a video. Tap Listen when you want the explanation. Stop, inspect the mechanism, answer a question, then continue.

01

Normal physiology: fuel is available

After a meal, rising glucose stimulates pancreatic β cells to release insulin. The important idea is the message: energy is available, use it and store it.

Muscle
↑ glucose uptake
↑ glycogen
→
Liver
↓ glucose output
↑ storage
→
Adipose
↓ lipolysis
↓ free fatty acids
02

Remove the signal

Without insulin, the body behaves metabolically as though it is starving despite abundant extracellular glucose. Two consequences now run in parallel: glucose accumulates and fat is mobilised.

Insulin ↓
→
Glucose pathway
hyperglycaemia → glycosuria → water loss
+
Fat pathway
lipolysis → ketones → acidosis
03

Predict the symptoms

Once the mechanism is clear, polyuria, thirst and weight loss stop being a memorised list. They are outputs you can derive from osmotic diuresis and a catabolic state.

04

Reverse the physiology

Treatment makes more sense when each intervention repairs a defined physiological problem. Insulin is needed not merely to lower glucose but to switch off lipolysis and ketogenesis.
