02 · Break the system

There are two obvious places to break this loop

Type 1

Lose the signal

Autoimmune beta-cell destruction → insulin falls → glucose uptake and fed-state signalling fail.

Core defect: absolute or near-absolute insulin deficiency.

Type 2

Resist the signal

Insulin is present, often high at first, but tissues respond less effectively. Beta cells compensate until they cannot keep up.

Core defect: insulin resistance plus progressive beta-cell dysfunction.

If insulin is high but glucose remains high, which failure fits best?

03 · Type 1 diabetes

No insulin means more than high glucose

Pancreas

Autoimmune destruction of beta cells removes the normal insulin response. In children this may become clinically obvious quite rapidly once beta-cell reserve is low.

Fuel state

Without insulin, hepatic glucose output continues and adipose tissue releases fatty acids. The liver converts fatty acids to ketones, creating the pathway to DKA.

04 · Type 2 diabetes

The pancreas compensates — until it cannot

Early

Muscle, liver and adipose tissue become less responsive to insulin. Beta cells raise insulin output, so glucose may remain near-normal for a time.

Later

The insulin requirement rises while beta-cell function progressively fails. Relative insulin deficiency appears on top of resistance and hyperglycaemia becomes sustained.

05 · Insulin resistance

The receptor can be occupied while the message is weak

INS

Signal chain

Insulin receptor→IRS-1→PI3K→Akt→GLUT4

Chronic energy excess can stress adipose tissue and promote inflammatory signalling that interferes with insulin signalling. The result is less effective GLUT4 recruitment in muscle and fat and less suppression of hepatic glucose production.

06 · Clinical features

Do not memorise the symptoms. Derive them.

Hyperglycaemia → glycosuria→

Filtered glucose exceeds renal reabsorptive capacity.

Glucose in urine→

Osmotic diuresis → polyuria.

Water loss→

Dehydration → polydipsia.

Poor fuel handling→

Catabolism + perceived energy deficit → weight loss, fatigue and sometimes polyphagia.

07 · Diagnosis

Prove hyperglycaemia. Then ask: is this child decompensating?

Establish diabetes

Use appropriate plasma glucose and/or HbA1c criteria in context. In a symptomatic child, a markedly raised random glucose is immediately important.

Do not miss DKA

Check ketones and acid-base status when clinically indicated. The urgent physiological question is whether insulin deficiency has driven ketogenesis and metabolic acidosis.

08 · Treatment logic

Treat the part of the physiology that is failing

Type 1

Replace insulin because the missing signal is the central defect. Basal insulin restrains fasting hepatic output; meal insulin handles incoming carbohydrate.

Type 2

Reduce glucose burden and insulin resistance, suppress inappropriate hepatic glucose output and use additional glucose-lowering pathways or insulin when needed.

09 · Reconstruct

Meal → insulin → signalling → GLUT4 → feedback

Normal: glucose rises → beta cells release insulin → receptor → IRS-1 → PI3K → Akt → GLUT4 → glucose uptake/storage → glucose and insulin fall.

Type 1: remove insulin. Type 2: weaken the response, compensate with more insulin, then lose sufficient beta-cell reserve. The symptoms and treatment now follow from the mechanism.
